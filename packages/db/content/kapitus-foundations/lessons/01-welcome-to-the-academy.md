# Welcome to the Academy

By the time you start this path, you've finished AI Basics. You know what a language model is, why fluent output isn't proof of accuracy, and why pasting the wrong thing into the wrong tool is the most common AI mistake people make at work. That foundation matters. Now we're going to make it specific to where you actually work.

Kapitus is a lender. Almost everything you touch on a given day involves someone else's money, someone else's credit history, and a set of regulators who care a great deal about how those things get handled. The AI Academy exists because we want our team to use AI well — not nervously, not blindly, but well — and "well" looks different at a lender than it does at a software startup or a marketing agency.

## Why we built this

Most AI training programs treat compliance as a list of "don'ts." That's not useful. A long list of prohibitions just makes people stop using the tools entirely, which is its own kind of failure — the work still has to get done, and someone on a competitor's team is doing it in half the time with AI help.

Our goal is the opposite. We want you to use AI confidently on the work where it's safe and high-leverage, and to know — quickly, without having to guess — when to stop and ask. The Academy is structured around the actual decisions you'll face on a given Tuesday afternoon: a draft going to an applicant, a spreadsheet of pipeline deals, a question from underwriting, an internal memo. Each path teaches you to recognize those situations and handle them the right way.

## What AI is good and bad at, _at a lender_

Generic AI training will tell you language models are good at drafting and bad at math. Both are true. But the picture is sharper at Kapitus.

**Where AI helps a lot at Kapitus:**

- Drafting non-sensitive correspondence — internal memos, sales follow-ups that don't quote applicant data, marketing copy, policy summaries for your own reading.
- Summarizing long documents you're already authorized to see — a 40-page operating agreement, a dense regulator guidance bulletin, a competitor's published rate sheet.
- Rephrasing and tightening your own writing. Take a clunky paragraph, ask AI to make it clearer, and you keep all the substance with none of the awkwardness.
- Brainstorming — questions to ask on a discovery call, possible objections from a broker, structure for a deck.

**Where AI is dangerous at Kapitus:**

- Anything that touches an applicant's personal information. SSNs, addresses, dates of birth, bank account numbers, credit scores. These are the regulated assets. We have rules about who can see them, where they can be stored, and who they can be sent to. Consumer AI tools are not on that list.
- Anything that makes a credit decision, or that looks like it could be making one. ECOA and fair-lending law care about the _process_ by which we decide, not just the outcome. AI-generated reasoning needs human ownership.
- Anything that goes out the door as a customer-facing legal notice — adverse action letters, disclosures, anything with regulatory triggering language. AI can help draft the boring parts; a human signs off on the legal parts.

The pattern: AI is a force multiplier on judgment work where the consequences live with you, and it's a serious hazard when it's between you and a regulated decision or a regulated data class.

## The four tiers

The Academy is structured as four levels. You don't have to do them all — most employees end somewhere between Practitioner and Specialist, and that's healthy.

**Apprentice.** Foundational. You've completed AI Basics and Foundations at Kapitus (this path). You can use the approved tools for non-sensitive work. You know which data class is which. You ask before you do anything novel.

**Practitioner.** Day-to-day fluent. You've completed your role's path (Sales, Underwriting, Servicing, Marketing, or Operations). You handle 80% of your AI use cases without supervision. You know what's outside your zone and you escalate cleanly.

**Specialist.** You go deeper in a single area — applicant-facing communications, credit memo drafting, marketing analytics, prompt engineering for the team. You can teach a colleague. You catch other people's mistakes.

**Hero.** A small handful of people. You're a force-multiplier for the whole company on AI — you propose new approved use cases, you sit in on policy revisions, you train new Specialists. The bar is high and the path takes months.

## What graduating means

Graduating from Foundations means three things:

1. You can name the four data classes (Public, Internal, Confidential, Restricted) and place a real Kapitus example into each one without thinking.
2. You can recite the 30-second version of what GLBA, ECOA, and FCRA require of you. You don't need to be a lawyer — you need to know what triggers a "stop and ask."
3. You've drafted at least one real, safe prompt for your own work and noticed what habits you want to build going forward.

## Day-1 expectations

Starting today, three things change:

- When you have an AI-related question about a real Kapitus situation — "can I paste this into ChatGPT?" — the answer is in this path or one phone call away. We'd rather you ask ten times in your first month than guess once.
- You start logging your AI use in the team channel for your role. Light-touch: which tool, what kind of task, anything you noticed. We learn from each other.
- You read the Kapitus AI policy at least once. It's short. The Academy is the explanation; the policy is the rule.

The point of this path is to make all of that feel obvious by the end. Let's get into it.
