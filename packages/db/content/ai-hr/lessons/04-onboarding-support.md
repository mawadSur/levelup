# Onboarding Support

Onboarding at most companies is either over-engineered — a week of back-to-back sessions that leave the new hire exhausted and no clearer on what they are actually supposed to do — or under-engineered, which is a laptop, a welcome email, and the vague instruction to "set up some 1:1s." AI cannot fix the organizational gaps that produce bad onboarding, but it can make the content creation side significantly faster and more specific.

## Personalized plans at scale

The standard 30-60-90 day plan is a useful frame, but most implementations are generic: "get familiar with the team," "understand the roadmap," "deliver first project." That language means different things to a new sales engineer, a new HR business partner, and a new finance analyst. The reason plans stay generic is that writing role-specific plans takes time HR rarely has.

AI cuts that time substantially. With the right inputs — role, team context, manager, and a few details about what the first deliverable is — it will produce a plan that is meaningfully more specific than what most teams put together manually.

**Prompt pattern:**

> "Generate a 30-60-90 day onboarding plan for a new [role] joining [team]. They start Monday. Their manager is [name and title]. Their first deliverable is [describe it]. Format as three phases with weekly milestones. Each week should include: two or three people they should meet and what to discuss, one or two things to learn or read, one concrete output or action. Be realistic, not aspirational — what actually moves the needle in a first quarter."

The "realistic, not aspirational" instruction matters. Without it, AI tends to produce plans that read like an organizational chart tour and a reading list, with no clear path to the new hire actually contributing something. The concrete output column is what keeps the plan honest.

## The questions new hires are embarrassed to ask

One of the highest-value prompts in HR is one most practitioners have not tried yet. New hires know things they do not know and, more usefully, they have questions they will not ask their manager because the questions feel too basic, too political, or too revealing of what they do not know yet.

**Prompt:**

> "You are a new hire starting in a [role] on [team] at a [size/type of company]. List 5 questions you would want answered in your first week but would be embarrassed to ask your manager directly. For each, explain why the question feels risky to ask."

The output will surprise you. Common results: how decisions actually get made (versus how the org chart says they do), what the unspoken rules are about calendar norms and after-hours messages, whether the previous person in this role was successful and why they left, who the real power brokers are outside of formal titles.

Take that list and go through it: which of these are actually answered in your onboarding documentation? Which require a human conversation? Building the answers to the non-sensitive ones into your onboarding materials removes friction that every new hire was quietly experiencing and never surfacing.

## AI Q&A for onboarding: the right tool for the right job

Some companies are experimenting with AI chatbots that answer onboarding questions — "where do I submit expenses," "how do I request PTO," "what is our policy on remote work." This can work well, but the quality of the answers is entirely dependent on the quality of the knowledge base behind it.

If you are considering this approach, use a company-approved tool that is connected to your actual HR knowledge base and that maintains appropriate access controls. Do not build or deploy a makeshift chatbot that routes questions to a public language model — even if you prompt it with policy documents, you cannot control what else it might say, and you have no audit trail for the answers it gives.

The simpler version that works now: take the most common onboarding questions HR gets in the first two weeks, write clean answers to each, and publish them in a searchable document or FAQ. That is not a chatbot, but it solves 80% of the same problem without the compliance exposure.

## What AI still cannot replace

No AI-generated onboarding plan replaces a manager who knows what a successful first quarter looks like and communicates that clearly in week one. No onboarding FAQ replaces a buddy who is genuinely invested in the new hire's success. No 30-60-90 template replaces honest feedback at the 30-day mark about what is going well and what needs to adjust.

AI helps with the content. The relationship work is still yours.

## Try this

Run the 30-60-90 prompt for either your most recent hire or an upcoming one. Then run the "embarrassing questions" prompt for the same role. Compare both outputs to what your current onboarding documentation actually covers.

You are looking for two things: gaps in what your materials explain, and whether the 30-60-90 plan AI produced looks like it would have actually set that hire up well. If the answer to the second question is "better than what we actually did" — that is worth acting on.
