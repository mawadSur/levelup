## The Email Your Prospect Deserves

Most cold emails fail before they are read. The subject line is generic, the first sentence is about the sender, and the ask buries itself in the third paragraph behind two sentences of flattery. Your prospect receives 80 of these a week. They delete them reflexively.

AI will not fix a bad strategy. But if you know what a good cold email looks like, AI can help you produce a solid draft in two minutes instead of twenty — and then you make it great.

## The Four-Part Structure That Works

Every high-performing cold email has the same skeleton:

1. **Trigger** — something specific that happened at their company or in their world
2. **Relevance** — why that trigger connects to what you sell
3. **Specific value** — a concrete outcome, not a feature list
4. **Soft CTA** — a single, low-friction ask

The email should be under 90 words. Your prospect reads on a phone between meetings. If it takes more than 30 seconds to read, most of them will not.

Here is what that looks like in practice:

> Saw that [Company] just brought on a new VP of Sales — congrats on the growth.
> When revenue leadership turns over, the first 90 days are usually about getting visibility into pipeline quality fast.
> We helped [similar company] cut their pipeline review time by 40% in the first quarter with a new VP.
> Worth a 15-minute call to see if that's relevant to you?

Trigger. Relevance. Specific value. Soft ask. No pleasantries.

## The AI Smell Problem

AI-generated cold emails have a recognizable pattern. Prospects have learned to spot it, and when they do, the email reads as lazy. Watch for these phrases in any AI output and delete them immediately:

- "I hope this email finds you well"
- "I wanted to reach out"
- "I came across your profile"
- "As a fellow [industry] professional"
- Any sentence that starts with "I" before establishing value
- Compliments that could apply to any company: "I love what you're doing at [company]"

These phrases signal that the email was not written for this person. They undermine the personalization you are trying to project.

## The Prompt That Works

This prompt consistently produces a usable first draft:

```
Write a cold email to a [title] at [company] who recently [specific trigger — e.g., raised
a Series B, posted 5 SDR job listings, published an article about pipeline predictability].

Reference this specific public detail: [quote or paraphrase the detail].

We sell [1-sentence description of your product/service].
The specific value we deliver: [concrete outcome — e.g., "AEs recover 4 hours per week
on manual CRM updates"].

Single ask: a 15-minute call.
Length: 90 words or fewer.
No filler phrases. No emojis. No "I hope this finds you well."
Write in a direct, peer-to-peer tone — not a pitch, a conversation starter.
```

Run this prompt, read the output critically, and then rewrite the weakest sentence. AI gives you a structure. Your knowledge of the prospect, the trigger, and the value makes it real.

## Personalization Is Not Optional

The personalization is not decoration — it is the mechanism by which the email works. A prospect who sees their own trigger reflected back at them knows you did your homework. That earns you 10 seconds of genuine attention. Those 10 seconds are all you need.

AI cannot invent personalization. It can only use what you give it. If you give it a generic trigger ("you seem like a fast-growing company"), the email will be generic. If you give it a specific trigger ("you published a LinkedIn post last week saying your team was drowning in manual data entry"), the email will be specific.

Your job before prompting is to find one true, specific, public thing about this person or their company. That takes two minutes of research. It is not negotiable.

## Writing Multiple Variants

One of AI's best uses in cold email is generating variants quickly. Instead of agonizing over one version, produce three:

```
Give me 3 versions of this cold email. Version 1: lead with the business trigger.
Version 2: lead with the specific value outcome. Version 3: lead with a direct question.
Keep all three under 90 words. Same CTA across all three.
```

Then pick the one that sounds most like you — not most like a sales email. A/B test if your email tool supports it, but even without a tool, having three options lets you choose based on instinct about what this particular prospect will respond to.

## The Edit Is the Work

Treat AI output as a rough draft, not a finished product. Read it out loud. If any sentence sounds like a pitch or a brochure, rewrite it. Ask yourself: would I say this to someone I met at a conference? If not, cut it.

The reps who use AI well in cold email are the ones who edit aggressively. The reps who copy-paste the output are the ones whose reply rates look exactly like average.

## Try This

Choose one prospect you have been meaning to reach out to but have not yet. Find one specific, public trigger (a news item, a job posting, a post they wrote). Draft three email variants using the prompt above, one for each framing. Read all three out loud and pick the one that sounds most like a conversation starter, not a pitch. Send it this week and note what worked.
